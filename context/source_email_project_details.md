## Project Summary

The project is a technical challenge/demonstration based on an RFP-style exercise. It uses intentionally corrupted data from 10 fictitious hospitals to test live proficiency in data cleansing, data mapping, transformation logic, governance, architecture diagramming, and demo communication.

## Required Work

1. Clean and normalize all 10 hospitals' data fields.
2. Correct corrupted fields.
3. Standardize inconsistent values.
4. Resolve illogical dates.
5. Normalize fields such as vendor names.
6. Ensure completeness where possible.
7. Produce one clean, consolidated national-level item master output file for consumable supplies usable at all 10 hospitals.
8. Summarize the approach, including business rules, assumptions, decisions, transformation logic, AI prompts, AI training, and AI tools used.
9. Explain where human-in-the-loop SME input and data governance are required for areas that cannot be normalized responsibly.
10. Explain how the approach scales to 200 hospitals for enterprise-wide deployment.
11. Produce SV-2, DIV-1, and DIV-2 diagrams reflecting the EHR to EIMS to FMS workflow and cleaned dataset.
12. Deliver a live, non-pre-recorded 30-minute virtual demonstration.
13. Respond to the same two Government-posed questions during a 15-minute Q&A.

## Evaluation Interpretation

- The exercise is a small-scale simulation of real SDLC and data-migration work.
- The evaluation is not only whether a clean file is produced, but whether the business rules and transformation logic hold up in a live walkthrough.
- AI usage must be disclosed as governed activity, not incidental tool usage.
- Scaling from 10 to 200 hospitals requires a repeatable, governed methodology.
- The live format tests whether the presenter can defend the approach in real time.
- The cleanfile, item master, and diagrams do not have a stated page limit, but the summary narrative is capped at 4 pages.
